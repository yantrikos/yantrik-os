# Channels: the desktop on your phone

*29 September 2026. Decisions by Pranab; P0 shipped in #493.*

Telegram, Signal, WhatsApp, Slack and the rest are **doors to the desktop**, the way Claude
Cowork's phone dispatch is a door to a desktop agent: you write from your phone, the mind that is
answering on your machine works on your machine, and what needs your word asks you where you are.

The rule is the one the Mind already keeps: **channels are doors, the person is the thread, the
mind is one.**

## Decisions

1. **The OS owns the channels.** One desktop identity per channel. A message is resolved to the
   person first, then becomes a turn to the *active* mind through the harness host, the same path
   the Lens takes, so the active mind, the hand-over, taint and Private mode hold for it. The
   Mind's family Telegram bot and Hermes' own bots keep running; they are theirs, not the
   desktop's door.
2. **Approval cards can be answered from a paired channel, except what cannot be undone.**
   Ordinary and sensitive acts can be allowed from the phone; anything `approvals::unrecoverable`
   (pay, send money, delete) waits for the machine.

## What exists (before P0)

`crates/yantrik-chat` has providers for Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack, Discord, Signal, Matrix, IRC,
and an unwired WebSocket server for a mobile app (`providers/native.rs`). All off by default.
Every inbound went to the built-in companion regardless of the active mind; nobody was
allowlisted on Signal; the per-conversation "no tools / not trusted" flags were never read; a
turn said nothing about where it came from; and approval cards could be answered only on the
machine's screen, expiring after about 110 s.

## P0 — only the person, directly, through the answering mind (#493, shipped)

- The router asks the AI only about a **direct message from someone in `chat.people`** (plus the
  Telegram chat the bot was set up with). Strangers and groups get nothing.
- The answer comes from the **active mind through the harness host**.
- WhatsApp's verify token is configured or random, never a fixed string.

## P1 — the turn says where it came from

**Origin on the turn.** `yantrik_harness::Turn` and the protocol's `Assignment` gain an optional
`origin`, absent for a harness that predates it:

```json
"origin": {"channel": "telegram", "remote": true, "person": "Pranab",
           "carries": ["text", "voice", "photo", "buttons"], "trust": "provider-readable"}
```

`channel` is `lens` for the desk. A mind uses it for register (terse on a phone) and for what to
send back (no 40-line table to a phone); a harness that ignores it is unaffected.

**Replies go out the door they came in.** Already so for a direct reply; P2 extends it to what
finishes later.

**Remote turns read; they change nothing, until P2.** A stolen phone or a hijacked Telegram
account must not be the person at the keyboard. While a turn from a phone is answered, the mind
answering it is held to `safe`: it reads, and anything that would change something is refused
with a sentence saying so, until P2 turns that refusal into a card on the phone. Not `standard`:
the security review of P1 found that for the built-in companion `standard` includes sending
email, overwriting a file, and starting sub-agents that escape the turn's cap.

**Only a mind that can be held answers a phone.**
- The built-in companion holds its own tools to `safe` for the turn, and what runs after it keeps
  that until the next message.
- A mind running as its own account is held through its token's reach on every door: the agent
  is made and held *before* its turn is queued, the turn goes only to the agent whose token was
  held, the hold is counted so overlapping turns end together, and while held the agent hands no
  work to another agent (whose own reach would not bind).
- A mind running as the person is held by nothing (it can drop its token or use its own shell),
  so it does not answer a phone; the phone is told why and what to do instead.

**The channels themselves.** IRC nicks cannot name the person (anyone can take one, over plain
text); a lone CR in a reply cannot smuggle an IRC command; an unknown Telegram chat type is a
group, not a direct message; a stranger's message is not even kept; nothing is kept while the
person is private; and the phone is told a sentence, never an internal error, since the channel's
operator may read it.

**Protocol change.** `origin` is additive and optional. yantrik-mind-72 is told before it reaches
VM 520.

## P2 — the phone answers cards, and results find you

*Cards on the phone: built (after P1's review). A phone turn's agent is held with `asks_above:
safe` and a `sensitive` ceiling: reads run, anything up to `sensitive` asks in every mode and no
desk session rule answers for it (`gate::Authority::asks_above`, Rust and Python), nothing above
runs. A card it raises goes to the phone with a one-time six-digit code
(`channels::card_raised`); `ALLOW 123456` / `DENY 123456` from the same identity on the same
channel answers it once within about two minutes. Unrecoverable acts are told to wait for the
machine; a provider-readable channel gets the card only as a notice unless named in
`chat.phone_approvals`. Phone turns are answered on a thread of their own through the router's
`Outbox`, so a mind waiting on the phone's Allow does not hold up the message that carries it.
The built-in companion stays read-only from a phone for now: its own tools have no card path.*

**Cards on the phone.** A card the remote turn raised is sent to the channel the turn came from:
what will run, in the card's own sentence, and Allow / Deny.

- Bound to that card id and a digest of its exact arguments, used once, expiring as the desktop
  card does.
- Accepted only from the person's identity on that channel, in the direct conversation.
- Never offered for an unrecoverable action: the phone says it waits for the machine.
- Telegram bot chats are readable by Telegram; an Allow there is only as strong as the person's
  Telegram account. So phone approvals are on for end-to-end channels (Signal, WhatsApp through
  whatsmeow, the paired app) and opt-in per channel for provider-readable ones.
- The audit line says where it was allowed from.

This deliberately changes the rule in `design/approvals-2026-09-21.md` that a card is answered
only on the screen: the answer still comes from the person, never from a mind, through a channel
the minds cannot write to.

**Presence-aware delivery.** At the desk, answers and notices go to the Lens; away (idle, locked,
or the last message came from a phone) they go to the channel the person last used. A long task
that finishes while you are out reaches you where you are.

## P3 — more doors

Signal, WhatsApp through whatsmeow (end-to-end to this box, no webhook host), Slack for work, and
the paired mobile app (`native.rs`: pairing code shown on the desktop, a key per device, push for
cards) as the most trusted door.

## Always

- **Private mode:** every channel answers only that Private mode is on. It cannot be left from a
  phone.
- **Channel trust labels:** a session that has read something private does not send it out on a
  channel less trusted than where it learned it (the ecosystem's labels, #482).
- **Nobody else:** a message from anyone not named is never shown to a mind, whatever the
  channel says about them.
