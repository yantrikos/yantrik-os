# Two apps came off the shelf

Written 20 September 2026, deciding the first two of the three Wave 3 questions in
`design/apps-plan-2026-09-20.md`. That plan says of Music, Network Manager and Spreadsheet: "the
honest options are to build the minimum real thing, or to remove the app from the launcher until
someone does — shipping the drawing is the option this plan rules out." Network Manager is being
built. Music and ySheets are shelved.

> **October 2026: ySheets is back on.** It came off the shelf in PR #727 with the five pieces
> listed under "ySheets — a week" below: a cell model that is always `rows × cols`, CSV open and
> save (through the desktop's FileChooser portal, with argv and `%f`), a formula engine with
> dependency-ordered recalculation and loop detection, SUM/AVERAGE/MIN/MAX/COUNT and more, and a
> control surface. Undo and redo came too. Everything else on the old screen was removed rather
> than backed. Music is still shelved.

Shelved does not mean deleted. Both crates stay in the tree and stay workspace members, so they
keep compiling and cannot rot in silence, and both keep their entry in the lints' debt. What
changed is that a person cannot reach them and a mind is not told they exist.

## What each app could not do

### Music

`apps/music-player/src/main.rs`, line 4, in the module doc:

> Playback engine integration (mpv) is stubbed for now.

That is the whole of it. There is no playback engine, no scanner, no library and no store. 2168
lines of Slint in `crates/yantrik-ui-slint/ui/music_player.slint` are driven by 312 of Rust, and
the Rust is mostly log lines: 23 of its 34 handlers do nothing, including every one that would
make a sound.

- Double-clicking a song is `app.on_play_track_index(|idx| { tracing::info!("Play track index {}
  (stub)", idx); });` — line 120. Next and previous are the same, on lines 63 and 64. So are the
  queue, the playlists and the folder watch.
- Nothing scans a folder, so the library is empty on every start. `is-scanning`, `scan-progress`
  and `scan-status` are `in` properties the Rust never sets: the screen draws a scanner that does
  not exist.
- The only way a track has ever appeared on that screen is `YANTRIK_MUSIC_DEMO=1` (line 184),
  which calls `demo::populate` and fills the library, the queue and now-playing with twelve
  invented tracks. It is honestly labelled a design fixture. It is also the only path to a
  photograph of this app working.
- There is no `.desktop` `%f`, no argv handling and no control surface, so neither a person nor a
  mind could hand it a file.

The lints see 10 unset `in` properties and 23 dead handlers. Both are in `baseline.json`.

### ySheets

`apps/spreadsheet/ui/app.slint` declares the model three times over:

```slint
in property <[SpreadsheetCell]> cell-grid <=> sheet.cell-grid;   // line 30
in property <int> row-count <=> sheet.row-count;                 // line 60
in property <int> col-count <=> sheet.col-count;                 // line 61
```

`in` means Rust writes them. Rust never does. So `cell-grid` is empty, `row-count` and
`col-count` are 0, and every handler that touches a cell begins by computing
`idx = row * cols + col` with `cols == 0` and then guarding `if idx < grid.row_count()` against a
grid with no rows. The guard always fails. `on_cell_clicked`, `on_cell_edited` and
`on_formula_submitted` — the three things a spreadsheet is — all take their early return.

The screen draws a perfect 50x26 grid. Nothing can be typed into any of its cells, by mouse or by
mind, and nothing ever could have been.

Behind that: 1249 lines of Slint over 156 of Rust, 44 of 47 handlers are log lines, there is no
formula engine at all (`is_formula` is `cell.text.as_str().starts_with('=')`, lines 57 and 77 —
the test for whether something *looks* like a formula, with nothing that evaluates one), and Save
and Load are `tracing::info!("Save sheet")` and `tracing::info!("Load sheet")` on lines 100 and
101.

The lints see 21 unset `in` properties and 44 dead handlers — the largest debt of any app in the
tree.

## What shelved means on this OS

One list, `SHELVED` in `crates/yantrik-ui/src/wire/dock.rs`, carrying for each app every name it
answers to, what it is called on screen, the binary it would have run, the reason it is not in
this build, and what would bring it back. Every surface consults it. The alternative — deleting
the two apps from each of seven places — is a shelf a future contributor undoes one line at a
time without ever deciding to.

The reason is a sentence a person reads, because it is quoted verbatim into the refusal. Asking
the shell to open Music now gets:

> `music` is not part of this build. Music is shelved: nothing plays audio yet — there is no
> playback engine, no scanner and no library behind the screen. It comes back when mpv is driven
> over its JSON IPC socket, a folder scan fills a small library store, and play/pause/next/queue
> and `open <file>` work — see design/shelved-2026-09-20.md. It can open: terminal, browser,
> files, settings, notes, editor, images, …

Not "Unknown app", which invites another spelling, and not "not installed", which invites an
install. Neither would have been true.

## Every place they were reachable from, and what happened at each

| Where | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `open_app` / `pin_app` on the shell's control surface | `check_launchable` passed; the app launched | Refused with the shelved reason above (`control.rs`) |
| The launcher's dispatch table, `wire::dock::ROUTES` | Two rows: `music`/`music_player` and `spreadsheet` | Both rows gone. `availability` answers `Shelved` before it looks at anything else |
| `on_launch_app` — tiles, pins, anything in the shell | Spawned the binary | Refuses and logs the reason, before any path that could spawn |
| The Apps launcher grid, the Lens's app results, `launchable_app_ids` | All read the catalogue | `Catalogue::refresh` drops a shelved entry, by id *and* by the program its Exec line runs |
| START pins | `spreadsheet` had a label and could be pinned | `publish` filters on `is_launchable`, which now says no. A pin already in `settings.yaml` stays there and stops drawing — un-shelve and it returns |
| The command palette | "Go to Music" (nav:20), "Go to Spreadsheet" (nav:29), "New Spreadsheet", "Import CSV" | All four gone, with the `spreadsheet:` dispatch arm. Screens 20 and 29 have no branch in `app.slint`, so those rows navigated to a blank screen even before this |
| The Lens's `resolve_action` | `launch:`/`exec:` ran whatever it was handed | Refuses a shelved id or binary, so a result that outlived the list it came from cannot start one |
| `.desktop` entries | `apps/desktop-files/yantrik-{music-player,spreadsheet}.desktop` were installed with the rest | Both stay in the repository; `build-release.sh` does not copy them into `share/applications` |
| The release bundle and the ISO | `build-release.sh` discovers every executable in `target/release`, so both shipped because they compiled | `SHELVED_BINS` excludes them by name, and the run prints "(shelved, not shipped: …)" |
| The companion's tools | `list_apps` and `describe_app` named `music` as an example app id | Both now say `weather` |
| `yos ls` | Lists live sockets | Neither app has ever published a control surface, so neither has a socket. Nothing to change |
| `describe shell` | Does not list installed apps at all | Nothing to change |
| Settings > default apps | There is no such screen | Nothing to change |

A test beside each: every spelling (`music`, `music_player`, `music-player`, `Music Player`,
`yantrik-music-player`, `spreadsheet`, `ySheets`, …) is refused with the shelved reason; the
catalogue filter drops a `.desktop` entry whose Exec runs a shelved binary even when the file is
on disk; no `ROUTES` row and no offered id names a shelved app; every shipped app is unaffected;
and `build-release.sh` is read from the Rust test, which fails if the shelf grows an entry the
packaging does not know about.

## What an installed machine still has on disk, and why that is safe

`yantrik-update apply` installs the new bundle's `bin/` **over** the old one, file by file. It
never removes a binary the new bundle does not carry. So a machine updated from a release that
had these apps keeps `/opt/yantrik/bin/yantrik-music-player` and `/opt/yantrik/bin/yantrik-spreadsheet`
indefinitely, and nothing in this change removes them. That is a wart, not a hazard, and it is
the same wart every binary ever dropped from this OS leaves behind. Building an uninstall path
is a separate piece of work.

`share/` is different: the updater mirrors it with `rsync -a --delete` (or `rm -rf` and copy
where rsync is absent), precisely so that "a file dropped from a release should disappear from
the machine too". The two `.desktop` entries therefore **do** go on the next update.

Neither of those is what the shelf relies on. The launcher's refusal was written for the state
the test VM is in right now — the binary present, the `.desktop` file present, the catalogue
finding it on every rescan:

- `availability` asks the shelf **before** it consults the catalogue, so a stale entry cannot
  answer `Ready`.
- The shelf is matched on the program an Exec line runs as well as on the app id, so an entry
  renamed or installed somewhere else is still caught.
- `entry_is_launchable` drops the entry during the scan, so it never reaches the launcher, the
  Lens or `open_app` at all.
- `on_launch_app` refuses again immediately before the spawn.

Running the binary directly from a terminal still opens it. That is true of anything on the disk
and is not something a launcher can or should prevent.

## The shell's own media screen is real; it is not these apps

Worth saying, because "Music is shelved" and "audio does not play" are different claims.

`crates/yantrik-ui/src/wire/media_player.rs` is screen 13, and it is real. `MpvHandle::spawn`
starts `mpv --no-video --no-terminal --input-ipc-server=/tmp/yantrik-mpv-<pid>` on the file; every
control writes a real JSON command to that socket and reads the reply. Play/pause is `["cycle",
"pause"]`, seek is `["seek", …, "absolute-percent"]`, volume, mute and speed are `set_property`.
A 500 ms timer polls `percent-pos`, `time-pos`, `duration` and `pause` and puts them on screen,
notices when mpv exits and resets the transport. Subtitles are read out of `track-list` and
selected by `sid`. A-B repeat seeks back for real. `start_playback` checks `has_command("mpv")`
first and writes "mpv not installed" onto the screen rather than pretending.

`mime_dispatch::classify` routes `.mp3`, `.ogg`, `.flac`, `.wav`, `.m4a`, `.aac`, `.opus` and
`.wma` to `FileAction::AudioPlayer`, which is this screen — it never routed to the Music app, and
it does not now. Nothing routes `.csv`, `.xlsx` or `.ods` anywhere near ySheets either: `.csv` is
in the text-editor list, and `.xlsx` and `.ods` are not classified at all, so they fall through to
the editor with everything else unknown.

What screen 13 does not have is a playlist — `on_player_next_track` and `on_player_prev_track` are
`tracing::debug!("not implemented for media player")` — a library, a scanner, or any effect for
its repeat and shuffle toggles, which flip a property and nothing else. So: one file at a time,
opened from Files, played for real. That is a smaller promise than the Music app made and it is
the only one this OS currently keeps. It was not touched by this change.

## What would bring each back

### Music — two to three days

The plan's minimum, unchanged, and every piece of it is already proven somewhere in this tree:

1. **mpv over its JSON IPC socket.** `wire::media_player::MpvHandle` is a working
   implementation — spawn with `--input-ipc-server`, write commands, poll properties, notice the
   exit. Lift it into a small crate both the shell screen and the app use, or copy it; do not
   write a third one.
2. **A folder scan into a library store.** Walk `~/Music` (and whatever the person adds), read
   tags, write a JSON or SQLite store under `~/.local/share/yantrik/music/`. The scan is the part
   `is-scanning`, `scan-progress` and `scan-status` were drawn for, so they get set for the first
   time.
3. **Play, pause, next, queue.** Four handlers that are currently four log lines, over a queue
   that is a `Vec` and a store that survives a restart.
4. **`open <file>`, argv and `%f`.** So Files, a person and a mind all hand it a file the same
   way, which is contract item 5.
5. **A control surface**, so `describe` says what is playing and the mind stops needing a
   screenshot.

Everything else on the 2168-line screen — playlists, folder watch, playback speed, the AI panel —
comes off until something backs it. That is contract item 7, and it is most of the work's value:
the app gets smaller.

Honest size: two to three days for someone who reads `media_player.rs` first. The scanner and the
store are the bulk of it; the playback half already exists.

### ySheets — a week, and it is the largest piece in the plan

1. **A cell model.** A `Vec<Cell>` in Rust, written into `cell-grid` with `row-count` and
   `col-count` beside it. This alone makes the grid typeable and turns three dead handlers live,
   because the guards they already have start passing.
2. **CSV load and save.** The narrowest real file format, and the one `Import CSV` in the command
   palette was already advertising. Then Save and Load stop being log lines, and argv and `%f`
   make it openable from Files.
3. **Arithmetic with references.** `=A1+B2`, `=A1*2`, precedence, a dependency order so a change
   recalculates what depends on it, and a cycle detected rather than hung. This is the piece with
   no precedent anywhere in the tree.
4. **SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX, COUNT over a range.** Five functions and a range parser, once 3 is
   working.
5. **A control surface** with `set_cell`, `get_cell` and `open`, so a mind can use it for the
   thing a mind would actually want a spreadsheet for.

Honest size: a week. The formula engine is most of it, and it is the one part nobody here has
built before. Charts, sort and filter, merge, freeze panes, find and replace, comments, number
formats and undo — the other 40 dead handlers — stay off the screen until the five above are
real.

## The count

Sixteen apps under `apps/`. Fourteen ship. The site says sixteen; it is now wrong by two.

The lints' shipping debt reads 105, down from 203, because the two shelved apps carry 98 of it.
That number is now true about what is on a machine, which is the point of separating them:
`tests/app-lints/shelved.toml` reports both apps under their own heading with the reason above,
leaves them out of the shipping totals, and does not fail the run. They are still linted. An app
named there that does not exist is an error, so the shelf cannot quietly outlive its apps.
