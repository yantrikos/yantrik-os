# Steers: correcting work while it is alive

17 Oct 2026. Pranab asked how a person gives feedback and changes course on work Yantrik is doing, "effortlessly", across every kind of work. This came out of a two-round discussion between Claude and GPT-6 Astra. It builds on [task contracts](task-contracts-2026-10-17.md).

## The principle

**You change the outcome you want. The OS keeps track of the consequences, the boundaries and the history.**

Today, correcting an agent means describing the problem in words in a chat box, explaining the context again, and repeating your preferences every time. That is the effort to remove. You point at the thing itself and say what you want, and the OS shows how it understood you. Your words are never rewritten, and every correction is visible from your words to the result.

What changes is less supervision per accepted outcome, not just fewer words or clicks.

## The primitive: a Steer

A Steer is a durable, versioned, scoped statement of a change you want, anchored to a piece of work. It isn't a chat message, and it never widens what the agent may do.

| Field | Contents | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | identity | the OS |
| `anchor` | task, contract revision, artifact version or execution event, the semantic target (*pricing page → annual toggle → mobile → signed out*), the captured context, a snapshot | the OS captures it; the person can retarget it explicitly |
| `expression` | your words, gesture, edit, example or chosen alternative, kept exactly as given | the person; immutable |
| `mode` | `question` · `explore` · `revise` · `preserve` · `stop` | the person, or proposed by the agent and labelled as such |
| `interpretations` | append-only: the change, its scope, its invariants and its uncertainties | the agent proposes; an edit by the person appends a new version |
| `conditions` | not before, a trigger, an expiry ("when the logo is ready", "launch week only") | the person, or an agent proposal |
| `authority` | author, role, the grant it falls under | the OS, from identity and the contract |
| `resolution` | state, candidates, conflicts, evidence, the person's decision | the agent and OS add evidence; only an authorised person decides |
| `events` | every transition, append-only | the OS |

Rules:
- **Lifecycle:** captured → interpreted → candidate or blocked → evidenced → accepted, rejected or superseded. A deferral is just a condition. **Stop skips interpretation and pauses at once.**
- **Storage:** Steers live in their own append-only store. A contract revision lists the Steers that apply to it.
- **A Steer that changes the goal** creates a contract revision before work continues. Approvals don't carry across the change.
- **On acceptance**, a Steer's agreed, testable constraints become scoped **checks** that later work must pass: "keep the footer" or "don't touch the headline" become regression checks. Subjective ones ("more playful") stay as review criteria; we don't invent brittle tests for them. An interpretation that hasn't been accepted is never an acceptance criterion.
- **The smallest useful scope by default.** "Shorter" means this paragraph unless you say otherwise. Nothing is learned as a lasting preference on its own.

## Read-back first, questions rarely

Every Steer immediately shows one editable line where you made it:

> **Reading this as:** hero visuals only · keep the headline · this page

Glancing at it costs nothing, and tapping any part of it corrects it. **A read-back is not consent.** The agent goes ahead without your acknowledgement only when the work stays within its existing grant, is local in scope and reversible, and leaves consequential choices open. Silence never widens the scope, teaches a lasting preference, or approves anything external.

**The OS may interrupt only when the next action needs your decision:**
1. the permission is missing;
2. materially different readings would change external effects, irreversible outcomes, sensitive access, or what gets accepted;
3. constraints that must hold conflict ("this pushes pricing below the fold: keep pricing visible, or the bigger hero?"), or the target can't be reliably found again.

Before interrupting, it first tries a safe candidate, defers the affected branch, or resolves the question from evidence it already has. If one of those works, the question waits for review time. If none works, it pauses only the affected action and asks the smallest question that tells the readings apart. Stop, or newly detected harm, pauses at once with a status, not a dialog.

**Boundary questions:** when a correction is ambiguous in a way that matters, the OS shows the nearest plausible misreading instead of asking an open question. "Warmer words too, or visuals only?" For an automation it shows past cases: "these three would also have stayed in your inbox under this reading". Rejecting one of them defines the boundary better than any rule you could write.

## The gesture for each kind of work

Every pointing gesture also works by keyboard, by voice, or from a list of objects.

| Work | Gesture |
|---|---|
| Visual (site, app screen, poster) | select a region or element and say the change; preview in the captured viewport and state |
| Writing | select a passage, edit it or direct it; a diff showing preserved facts and scope |
| Invisible logic (scripts, back ends) | a corrected example plus a counterexample; the inferred rule previewed against boundary cases |
| Research and choices | "more or less like this", with the inferred dimension shown (cheaper? direct?) before reranking |
| Background automations | correct one past outcome; the proposed rule replayed on past cases before it goes live |
| Plans and schedules | move a milestone or storyboard frame; downstream changes shown |
| Data and models | flag a result, then correct its inputs, assumptions or transformation, not just the number shown |
| Multi-step workflows | steer a transition or gate: "never send before I review", "ask me only above ₹5,000" |
| Irreversible actions | steer the rehearsal and its checkpoints before running; afterwards, mitigation, not a pretend undo |

For big structural work, the agent may show a short **storyboard** first (wireframes, an outline, a timeline), so a correction lands on a sketch instead of on a finished build. It is a proposal, not a gate on every tweak.

**Pause affected work** is always visible. It stops new actions at once, cancels what can be cancelled, and states honestly what is already running or already visible.

## Week 1 on the live machine

One project at a time, visual corrections only. The goal is to prove the loop, not the whole model.

- **The overlay** on the served project:
  - select an element or the page, and type;
  - **Try change**, **Ask** and **Keep this**, plus an always-visible **Pause affected work**;
  - the editable read-back;
  - before and after with the check results;
  - **Accept correction** or **Reject**.
  - It captures the route, viewport, UI state, element identity, a screenshot and the source revision. If the element can't be found again, it blocks and asks for a new target; it never guesses.
- **The Director:**
  1. stores the Steer (SQLite plus an event log);
  2. resolves its scope against the mission's grant;
  3. has the Mind build it in an isolated worktree with its own preview;
  4. runs the project's checks plus every accepted Steer's checks;
  5. shows the evidence and the candidate;
  6. promotes the candidate only on acceptance, and only within the grant.
  - The week-1 checks are deliberately narrow: preserved copy is unchanged, the preserved target still exists, basic interactions pass a smoke test, and nothing overflows the viewport.
- **The stream** shows:
  - the promoted project;
  - a small status: *Correction queued · Building candidate · Check failed · Ready for review*;
  - optionally, a labelled before and after.
  - **It never switches to a candidate on its own**, because showing a draft publicly is an external effect. Private notes, raw logs and unrelated desktop content stay off it.
- **Worked if:** ten varied corrections run, plus a few matched corrections through chat, with these results:
  - every correction can be traced from expression to candidate to decision;
  - at least one deliberately planted regression is caught before promotion;
  - the pause drill and the stale-anchor drill pass;
  - nothing is promoted without authorisation, and no private note is exposed.
  - Measure the person's correction time, restatements, collateral changes and time to acceptance. Ten corrections prove feasibility, not reliability.

## How we would know this is wrong

Run a two-week crossover on Pranab's real work: website revisions, writing, one automation, research and scheduling. Compare Steers with chat on matched corrections, alternating the order. Measure:
- active time to an accepted correction;
- how often he restates a correction;
- changes outside the intended scope;
- interruptions;
- accepted constraints broken later;
- whether he can say correctly what will change and what is still unauthorised.

The bar: **30% less median time**, fewer restatements, no more collateral changes, and no unauthorised external action. **We are wrong** if:
- the interaction gets faster while review gets slower;
- he regularly opens chat to explain a Steer;
- he likes the demos but avoids Steers on work that matters.

## Cut, for now

- Mandatory storyboards (use them only for structural uncertainty).
- Inferring feedback from undos or abandonment, and a "noticed" tray: too ambiguous and too private.
- Automatic preference learning: accepted, scoped constraints are enough.
- Automatic counterfactual generation as its own subsystem: probes stay available as needed.
- Universal gesture infrastructure, a UI for collaboration roles, and running recurring conditions in week 1.
- "Every correction becomes an automated test": every accepted correction becomes a scoped commitment, and only some of them can be tested mechanically.

## A scene

Pranab opens the site on his phone, selects the hero and types: "Make this playful. Don't touch the headline." The chip reads **Hero visuals only · keep the headline · this page.** He keeps browsing while the Mind builds a candidate. The Director flags: **The purchase-button check failed at mobile width. Candidate not promoted.** The Mind fixes the layout and runs the checks again. At review he sees the before and after: warmer colours, illustrated shapes, the headline unchanged, the purchase button working. He taps **Accept correction**, the candidate is promoted under the existing publishing grant, and the stream updates.
